### Ikigai: a Japanese reason for being

**Ikigai** (生き甲斐) is a Japanese word made of *iki* (life) and *gai* (worth, value). It means roughly **"a reason for being"** — what makes you want to get up in the morning. In Japan, ikigai does not have to be a job: it can be tending a garden, cooking for your family, or teaching the kids next door. The people of Okinawa, one of the places where people live longest, are often described as having a strong ikigai.

In the West, ikigai was later drawn as **four overlapping circles** to think about careers. That diagram is a modern interpretation (it became popular around 2014), not the original Japanese meaning — but it is a very useful map for understanding yourself:

<Ikigai lang="en" />

A few things to remember when you use this map:

- **Nobody finds the centre straight away.** At 18, what matters is knowing which circles are strong for you and which are still missing.
- **The "good at" circle grows.** Most skills come from practice, not talent.
- **There is one more factor: your situation** — your family, your budget, where you live. That is not a weakness to hide. It is information that helps you plan honestly.

A useful tool for the "love" and "good at" circles is the **Holland test**. It sorts interests into six types — Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising and Conventional (RIASEC) — and suggests majors and jobs that match. It takes about five minutes.

## 4. Two directions worth a closer look

Two directions come up often for young people: **becoming a teacher** and **learning Korean**. They are also a good example of how one interest can lead to several paths.

### Becoming a teacher

If you love explaining things to others (a strong **Social** type), teaching may suit you. Teacher-education students can receive state support for **tuition plus 3.63 million VND a month** for living costs. In return, you must work in education for at least twice the length of your training, starting within 2 years of graduating — otherwise the support must be paid back ([details](https://hoatieu.vn/phap-luat/hoc-phi-sinh-vien-su-pham-215216), [conditions](https://thuvienphapluat.vn/phap-luat/sinh-vien-su-pham-co-phai-boi-hoan-kinh-phi-ho-tro-tien-dong-hoc-phi-va-chi-phi-sinh-hoat-khi-bi-ky-572356-152468.html)).

### Learning Korean

Korean opens several doors, and they don't exclude each other:

1. **Korean language at university** → interpreter, office jobs at Korean companies in Vietnam (South Korea is one of the biggest foreign investors here).
2. **Study abroad in Korea** → language school (D-4 visa), then university (D-2), with part-time work allowed.
3. **Work in Korea through the EPS programme** → from age 18, after passing the EPS-TOPIK Korean test.

Whichever you choose, **starting Korean now costs nothing and helps every option.**

## 5. If you think about working abroad

Working abroad can change a family's situation — and it can also go badly wrong if you trust the wrong people. Before anything else, decide **why** you want to go and **what you will do after** the contract ends. Then protect yourself:

<CareerLists lang="en" kind="scams" />

<Callout type="tip" title="How to check a company">
  Every licensed labour-export company is listed by the Department of Overseas Labour at [dolab.gov.vn](http://dolab.gov.vn/New/Default.aspx) → *Doanh nghiệp XKLĐ* → *Danh sách*. Type the company's exact name. If it is not there, walk away. Korea's EPS programme is organised by the two governments and announced through your local labour office, not by brokers.
</Callout>
